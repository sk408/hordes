# HORDES: review and improvement plan (November 2026)

**What was reviewed:** the live game, GitHub `main` at `ce40d72` (3 October 2026). Nothing has shipped since. Screenshots, a full run on desktop and phone, the four movies, every menu, and an offline render of every sound and the whole music track were captured from that exact build on 16 November. Eight reviewers on Opus each covered one area. A fact-checker re-checked the 18 serious findings: 13 confirmed, 5 partly right, none wrong. A planner turned the findings into releases, and a critic checked the plan. The corrections are folded in below.

## The short version

- **Players are telling you three things, and all three are still true in the live game:** the level-up cards pick themselves after 6 seconds, there is no straight flush, and the music and sounds are thin. All three are cheap to fix.
- **Returning players get a bad first moment.** Anyone who played before October gets the old "THE DRAFT: pick 1 card" tip at their first level-up, under a dark overlay that hides two of the three cards. One line of code.
- **You are right that art, music and movies are the easy wins, with one twist.** The cheapest art wins are drawing code, not new sprites: from minute 10 the fight is unreadable, the hero gets lost, the stages are one dark floor in eight tints, and the characters never turn to face where they walk. Fixing those lifts everything drawn later.
- **Music needs real files.** I can't hear, so tuning the synth blind would recreate the sound players rejected. The plan keeps the synth as a fallback and brings in music and core sounds you approve on a one-page listening tool (about 30 minutes of your time).
- **The movies are cheap to fix** because the escape chase already has the renderer the others need. The death movie (seen every run) and the portal movie (every wave) are the weakest and the most-seen.
- **The game has plenty of systems but thin decisions inside a run.** That is the next phase, not this one, except for one question for you about weapon offers in drafts.

## What each area found

### Art

- **From minute 10 the fight is unreadable.** Enemy shots, gems and damage numbers 30-90 px tall cover most of the screen. Enemy bolts use the same violet and green as the gems, so danger looks like reward.
- **The hero gets lost late,** worst on phones: numbers are drawn over the hero, and three of the four pilots share colours with enemies, shots or numbers (that colour overlap is not yet verified).
- **The eight stages are one dark, flat floor in eight tints.** Snowfield and Whiteout have no white in them; Ashen Waste and Snowfield are nearly the same slate blue. This is why a player said "different arenas are barely noticeable".
- **Characters are stiff.** Nobody turns to face where they walk, the two animation frames often differ by a pixel or two, and bodies vanish into a puff when killed.
- **Nothing in the game is lit.** No glow, no light from braziers or windows, no night. The games you admire (Balatro, Ball x Pit) get much of their look from effects, not sprite detail.
- **The finale and bosses lack spectacle,** and the tone reads as cute toy rather than threat.
- Keep: the crisp 1x actors with dark outlines, the stage buildings (the best art in the game), the title painting.

### Music and sound effects

- **The music** is square bass, triangle lead and a noise hat: short blips with no held notes, chords, filters, reverb or stereo. Each section uses only 3-6 distinct bars.
- **It is very quiet and uneven:** music averages -48 dB; combat sounds peak around -42 dB while movie roars hit -21 dB, with nothing limiting them.
- **Most of the game is silent:** title, menus, shop, camp, all four movies, death and victory have no music.
- **The most-heard sounds never vary,** and the hit sound is a harsh 10 kHz hiss.
- **Sounds don't carry meaning:** an empty chest plays the reward fanfare; hands, crits, low health and elite arrivals make no sound.
- **Sourcing traps:** free tiers of AI music and sound tools don't allow commercial use, and the Sonniss packs probably can't sit raw in a public repo. Kenney's packs are CC0.

### Movies

- **The death movie is the weakest and the most-seen** (every run that ends early, and run 1 is meant to). A stranger in an old palette, blown up 8x among grey blobs on black, almost silent, slowed to 0.7x, then a 12-line text wall.
- **Wave 1 tells the same story twice:** the portal movie (hero walks into a portal) then the escape chase (hero dives through a portal). About 16 seconds in every run, three minutes in.
- **The portal movie plays after every wave** with the wrong boss (always Gravelmaw), always the Knight, two pixel sizes in one frame, and held pauses.
- **The intro is 7 seconds of grey silhouettes on black on every page load,** then hard-cuts to a much better title painting it never connects to.
- **The escape chase is the model:** the equipped pilot, the enemies you met, the real boss and stage props on a road, film bars and cued sound. Its renderer already takes stage, pilot, enemies and boss, so the others become new timelines on existing code.

### Menus and HUD

- **Late in a run the HUD is about 20 separate text panels plus a 12-chip key strip,** and damage numbers sit on the hero.
- **Evolution, the best moment of a run, is two text buttons on a black screen.** The end of a run is a 13-line text dump.
- **The playing cards are thumbnails, not objects you'd want,** and your poker hand is never shown on screen.
- **Two type systems:** menus use the system monospace font; the HUD uses pixel type. Every menu tile has the same weight, so nothing reads as the main button.
- **Settings: 21 controls over two pages,** and the grid overflows its own panel. A few clipping and overlap problems on phone and portrait.

### First session and returning players

- **New player:** about 15-25 seconds from page load to the run, first kill within seconds, first real choice 40-60 seconds after load. The guided run teaches by doing, as intended.
- **Most likely reasons to bounce in the first three minutes:** a silent, cheap-looking first 10 seconds (the intro), a slow guided start (15-second idle waits), the blacked-out first draft for returning players, cards auto-picked before they can be read, and thin audio.
- **The returning-player bug:** every profile from before October that closes the what's-new note gets the old coach card at its first draft. Its spotlight is placed once and misses the cards. It also hits anyone who uses REPLAY TOUR.

### Game depth

- **Drafts never offer a new weapon,** so the build is fixed in the menu before the run. The captured 15-minute run had both its weapons maxed and evolved by 5:20, then spent 20 drafts on stat cards.
- **The poker hand fills itself** from whatever is drafted and ends up at the same top hands.
- **Stages differ in numbers, not in play;** the same bosses rotate everywhere, and all six boss rules change numbers only. The midboss came four times in 15 minutes.
- **The four characters are stat variants,** and after hour three the chase is mostly bigger numbers.
- These are real, but this plan doesn't take them on, except the weapon-offer question (decision 7), which your September decision made.

### Loading and delivery

- **Loads fine:** 91 modules, about 0.9 MB compressed, no broken requests; frame time is fine at 250-300 enemies.
- **No loading text and no error screen.** On a slow phone, or in the minutes after an update when old and new files can mix, the player sees a black screen.
- **Every GitHub Pages deploy makes returning players re-download everything.** Harmless at 0.9 MB, but 5-15 MB of audio loaded the plain way would be re-downloaded on every release.
- **The safe way to add audio:** load it after the first frame, one track at a time, cached under names that change only when the file changes, with the synth as the fallback if anything fails.

## The direction: one style

**Lit pixel dark fantasy on a card table.**

- **Keep** the crisp 1x pixel actors with dark outlines, the gold-on-brown tile kit, the title painting, the stage buildings and the escape movie.
- **Light the world:** stages with their own light and value (snow stages genuinely light, lava and void glowing), torches and shots that cast light, a muted dark-fantasy palette.
- **Three colours are reserved:** red-orange for danger, cyan-teal for reward, warm white for the hero. On a full screen the order of visibility is hero, threats, enemies, pickups, numbers last.
- **Motion over detail:** actors face where they move, bob, squash when hit and collapse when killed.
- **Cards look like cards,** Balatro-style, in one pixel font.
- **Audio sounds produced, not raw:** dark chiptune/synthwave with filters, pads, real drums and some space. UI sounds are cards and paper; combat sounds are short and varied; movie stings sit at most 6 dB above combat.
- **All movies share the escape's camera:** the road, the film bars, and your own pilot, stage, enemies and boss. Anything that repeats every run lasts under 4 seconds and skips the same way.

**How each part gets made:**

- **Art:** drawing code first. It costs nothing in assets and I can check it with before/after sheets and measurements. Small sprites stay as code grids. About ten large showpieces (the Maw, close-ups of the pilots and bosses for movies) come from an image generator you run or pay for; I snap them to the palette and import them as grids. A wholesale sprite replacement only if a one-day comparison sheet beats the improved originals.
- **Audio:** hybrid. The mix, variation and meaning fixes are code I can measure offline. The music and core sound effects come from files (Kenney CC0 as a base, plus files you generate, buy or commission), chosen by you on a listening page. The synth stays as the fallback. About 5 MB in total.
- **Movies:** in the engine, no video files. Death, portal and intro are rebuilt on the escape's renderer.

## Decisions for you

Each had a recommended default. All nine were settled on 2026-11-17 (Steve's answers are quoted where he gave more than "recommended"); only decision 7's hand-size question is still open.

1. **Draft auto-pick. Decided 2026-11-17.** Two switches on the draft screen, both remembered: AUTO-PICK ON/OFF (key U) and WAIT, which steps 6s down to 1s and wraps (key T). The default stays ON at 6 seconds, so idle players still see their build grow, and nothing is instant. The evolve offer follows the same switches. With auto-continue on and AUTO-PICK OFF, the draft waits and auto-continue takes it after 30 seconds. Later: pick by build instead of at random.
2. **Straight Flush payout. Decided 2026-11-17: as recommended.** Ranks above Four of a Kind; pays Four's bonus plus the Straight's faster cooldown, plus the suit bonus. Royal Flush and Five of a Kind can wait.
3. **Music and sound source and spend. Decided 2026-11-17: music from Google Lyria.** Steve found Lyria 3 (Gemini API), set up the API key and billing ($5 plus $30 of credit) and said "we're working right now". Music is made with Lyria 3 Pro (about $0.08 a song; capped at $8 for this round), cut into loops and mastered here; Lyria RealTime (free, experimental) is the backup. Sound effects: Kenney's CC0 packs as the base, gaps settled in R4. Steve picks the tracks by ear on a listening page.
4. **Audio and font files in the public repo. Decided 2026-11-17: as recommended.** Yes, under `assets/` with a CREDITS file, plus one free (OFL) pixel font, which I pick (Steve: "recommended action"). Imported art becomes code grids, not image files.
5. **Art tone. Decided 2026-11-17: as recommended.** Muted dark fantasy, menacing bosses and Maw, light snow stages, a warm Bone Desert, with red-orange danger, cyan-teal reward and warm-white hero reserved. Nothing ships until you approve the before/after sheets. Terrain relief (Steve: "whatever you recommend"): the flattening was his own rollback on 2026-09-18 ("It needs more work before we put it back"), and the authored plateaus of `src/terrain.js` now carry elevation in play. Recommended and adopted: keep relief off for movement; in R6, try it back as light and shadow only (snow drifts, dunes), judged on the before/after sheets.
6. **Showpiece art source. Decided 2026-11-17: my call** (Steve: "whatever you recommend. can source more too"). The art research found what looks like your own Qwen-Image setup; it is the recommended source for the ten or so large pieces, if its licence allows (to confirm). A paid pixel-art service (PixelLab or Retro Diffusion) only if it falls short.
7. **Weapons in drafts. Decided 2026-11-17.** Steve: "i think i intended to have potential weapon loadout in the menu, i didn't mean for it to actually give those weapons to the player. just hadn't gone back to fixing it." So the loadout lists the weapons a run can offer; the run starts with one weapon and the rest arrive as draft cards (R1, below). Still open: the fixed 5-card hand. The original question: in September you moved weapon choice to the menu before the run. Since then, drafts never offer a weapon, and builds are complete by minute 5. Do you want new-weapon cards back in drafts, perhaps rarely? The answer changes what the R5 cards must hold, so it is needed before R5. (Also: a fixed 5-card hand, Balatro-style, instead of every card joining?)
8. **Auto-continue penalty. Decided 2026-11-17: my call** (Steve: "whatever you recommend"). Recommended and adopted: no penalty for the first 3 unattended runs in a row (a coffee break), then 25% less gold instead of 50%; the 20-run cap stays. The original question: is 50% less gold with a 20-run cap a firm choice, or can it be 25%, or start after a few runs?
9. **Calm synth music under the title and movies in R1. Decided 2026-11-17: no, as recommended** (moot now: the Lyria tracks cover the title, menus and movies).

## Risks

- **R4 depends on files you source and approve.** R1 to R3 need no assets, so they are not blocked.
- **More AI-made assets on a gen-ai-tagged 2.5-star page.** The commenter accepts AI but rejects weak output, so your listening and look sessions are the quality gate, and the store's disclosure should say what changed.
- **I can't hear.** Audio work is checked by offline measurement (loudness per category, peaks, loop seams, size); your listening sessions are hard gates and the synth fallback stays permanent.
- **Licences.** Free AI tiers are non-commercial, raw Sonniss files may not be redistributable, and the repo has no LICENSE file. Only CC0 or paid files go in, each with a CREDITS row. The art research also found shipped source comments that cite commercial game assets as references; R4 removes them.
- **Phones.** Light, glow and new art may cost frame time with 266 enemies on screen; every art release is gated on a phone performance check with an automatic fallback. iOS audio behaviour must be tested on your phone.
- **The draft countdown change touches a pinned test and the idle design.** Unattended runs must keep picking at about 6 seconds and never park on a draft.
